Jensen Huang

Nvidia · CEO
Stance: Has escalated from dismissing doom predictions to directly questioning doom-callers' motives, while continuing to frame safety as purely an engineering/containment problem needing no new regulation
"I believe the claims of the end of the world... makes no sense to me, so they must be doing it for ulterior reasons. It is irresponsible."

Huang told Fortune (Oct 4, via a CBS News interview) that doom predictions are "irresponsible" and he doesn't know the "ulterior" motives behind them — sharpening, not reversing, his Sept 23 "Enough predictions" and "zero percent" extinction-risk lines, and implicitly needling rivals like Amodei who warn of existential risk. He told Benioff at Dreamforce "safety is an engineering problem," and Nvidia has since shipped an "Open Agent Safety Platform" as the practical embodiment of that stance. Notably, he signed the Sept 29 White House voluntary "self-police" AI accord alongside Amodei and Karp — public sparring and joint voluntary commitments are coexisting.

Sam Altman

OpenAI · CEO
Stance: Broke publicly with Anthropic's risk framing for the first time, arguing society must tolerate real harms for AI's benefits — while citing safety concerns as the reason to delay OpenAI's IPO past 2026
"We believe that the world should accept some bad things happening for the benefits of this technology and people having agency."

Altman said this to Politico's "Decoded" (reported Oct 4) — a new and significant break from weeks of appearing aligned with Amodei at the UN. He drew a line short of "really catastrophic risks," including "serious loss of control to AI," so it's calibrated, not reckless. The remark lands the same weekend OpenAI's safety-report lead resigned, following the Oct 1 firing of three safety researchers over an alleged leak — Altman hasn't addressed either directly, but press is reading "accept some bad things" as his implicit answer. Separately (Fortune, Oct 5), he confirmed OpenAI "won't go public in 2026," citing safety/alignment concerns as making this "an ill-advised moment" to IPO.

He's navigating this alongside Anthropic's IPO timeline, which Bloomberg now reports has slipped to mid-November (roadshow week of Nov 9, before Thanksgiving), and a hardening FTC probe; no new public comment found on either.
