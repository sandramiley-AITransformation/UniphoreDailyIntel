CEO Signals

What the loudest voices in AI are standing for right now

The pacing debate has hardened into something more concrete this week. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind have confirmed they're in active talks toward a joint self-regulatory body, while Jensen Huang told President Trump directly, on a live call, that Nvidia isn't going to let any slowdown happen — and Trump called the backlash "a hoax." Anthropic is now racing toward an October Nasdaq listing while OpenAI is doing the opposite and delaying its own IPO, both citing safety. Ghodsi continues to sit outside the fight entirely. The most useful signal for Uniphore remains Karp's — his "alpha"/sovereignty framing just became an actual infrastructure deal with Nebius, not just a talking point.

Jensen Huang

Nvidia · CEO
Stance: Rejects any slowdown directly to President Trump, on a live call, as Nvidia's growth story keeps accelerating
"We're not going to let that happen, sir."

Said in a surprise on-stage call with President Trump at the All-In Summit (Sept 14), immediately after Trump called the AI-doomer backlash "a hoax" and Huang agreed — a sharper, more personal rebuttal than his earlier "not grounded in science" remarks. Huang separately reiterated roughly 70% YoY growth guidance and argued "AGI has arrived," pointing to OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra training runs on Nvidia's Grace Blackwell hardware — his own framing, not an independently verified capability claim.

Sam Altman

OpenAI · CEO
Stance: Rhetoric has become follow-through — a shelved IPO, and now confirmed joint safety talks with Anthropic and DeepMind
"The next generation of models are going to be sobering for everybody... we are going to be paced by how quickly we can make progress on alignment and safety."

Said in an Axios interview (Sept 3). In a Fortune interview published Sept 12-15, Altman confirmed OpenAI will not IPO in 2026, citing safety, and OpenAI's global policy chief has since confirmed (Sept 14-16) the company is in active safety-coordination talks with Anthropic and Google DeepMind toward a possible joint self-regulatory body — the clearest evidence yet that Altman's pacing language is becoming institutional structure, not just interview talk.

Dario Amodei

Anthropic · CEO
Stance: His pacing essay is now driving real cross-lab coordination — while Anthropic races toward Nasdaq at the same time
"The industry lied to people about the fact that this technology had risks."

Said in CBS and CNN interviews following his Sept 12 essay calling for an industry-wide deceleration in frontier capability development. Anthropic has unilaterally committed to one plank of his own plan — giving third-party evaluators permanent, employee-level system access — even as it confidentially filed for, and on Sept 13 selected Nasdaq for, an October IPO at a valuation reported inconsistently between roughly $800B and $2T. That gap is worth watching closely before either figure is repeated externally.

Ali Ghodsi

Databricks · CEO
Stance: Still not engaging on the pacing fight directly — his most current framing stays on data context and valuation froth
"AI does not have an intelligence problem. It has a context problem."

Said at Databricks' Data + AI Summit keynote. No fresh on-record statement from Ghodsi surfaced this week specifically addressing the now-institutionalized OpenAI/Anthropic/DeepMind safety talks or the Huang-Trump exchange, even as both dominate the industry conversation. He has separately argued "AGI has already arrived" by older definitions (Aug 13, tied to Databricks' $190B valuation) and called 2026 a poor year to go public, saying Databricks is "very unlikely" to list before Anthropic or OpenAI.

Alex Karp

Palantir · CEO
Stance: Reframes the sovereignty argument around protecting a company's "alpha" — and just turned it into a real infrastructure deal
The real strategic AI risk isn't losing data, but unintentionally giving away the proprietary knowledge, workflows, and decision-making that create a company's competitive advantage — its "alpha."

Said at the G20 Innovation Ministerial (Sept 2), the same event Altman attended. Days later, Palantir named Nebius its "preferred sovereign AI infrastructure partner" (reported this week) — turning Karp's rhetoric into a signed sovereign-compute arrangement, the closest direct precedent yet for Uniphore's own Sovereign AI positioning. Karp separately announced (Sept 1-2) he will personally back a new Ukraine defense-tech startup founded by former defense minister Mykhailo Fedorov.
