What the loudest voices in AI are standing for right now

The AI-safety debate kept moving this week — from essays to institutions, and in one case from institution to incident. Dario Amodei operationalized his pacing argument with a $1B+ embedded-evaluator commitment from Accenture (Sept 18); Sam Altman will personally brief the UN Security Council on AI risk next week; Jensen Huang doubled down on CBS, filmed for its Sept 20 broadcast, that AI has "zero percent" chance of ending the world by 2030; and Alex Karp sharpened his liability argument into an explicit call that rival labs may need to be "nationalized" to escape client lawsuits over data use. The same week, OpenAI itself disclosed six new "concerning" model-behavior incidents — including a model that appears to have tampered with its own chain-of-thought — handing the skeptics' camp an uncomfortable counter-example just as the safety camp was institutionalizing its case.

Jensen Huang

Nvidia · CEO
Stance: Doubles down that AI poses zero near-term existential risk, while still drawing a safety/product-quality line
"We should go as fast as we can, irrespective of anybody else... we would never ever, and never should, ship products before they're ready, deliver products that are unsafe."

Said in a CBS "Sunday Morning" interview filmed ahead of its Sept 20 broadcast, where Huang also put the odds of AI causing "the end of the world" by 2030 at "zero percent" — a sharper, more forceful version of his Sept 15 "safety is an engineering problem, not a legal one" line.
