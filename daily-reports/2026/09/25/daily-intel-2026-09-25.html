Key themes & hot topics on the horizon

  • Model access is becoming a geopolitical lever, not just a commercial one

    The US directing OpenAI and Anthropic to withhold models from a close ally's own safety institute previews a world where enterprise buyers must ask not just "which model" but "will we keep access to it." Expect data-residency and access-continuity guarantees to become standard procurement questions well beyond China-specific concerns.

  • Frontier labs are building their own regulator before governments finish building theirs

    Reports that Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic are quietly forming a voluntary, FINRA-style "Frontier AI Standards Agency" suggest the labs would rather self-police than wait for binding government rules. Watch whether this body gains real teeth or functions mainly as a lobbying shield — either way it will shape what "responsible AI" means for enterprise buyers.

  • Chinese AI provenance risk is compounding across regulatory, legal, and intelligence fronts simultaneously

    A US joint cybersecurity advisory, a narrowing Chinese government probe, and an escalating corporate legal dispute are now all live at once, each reinforcing the others' credibility. Expect enterprise buyers to demand contractual proof of data handling from any vendor touching Chinese models, directly or through an aggregator, well before any of these threads formally resolves.

  • The "agent context and evaluation" layer is commoditizing faster than the model layer did

    Databricks, Snowflake, and Glean are all absorbing capability — spreadsheets, evaluation tooling, connector management — that used to differentiate specialist vendors. Vertical CX depth, not generic agent tooling or model access, is becoming the only defensible ground left for application-layer vendors like Uniphore.

A vendor seen as captive to one model provider's politics or business terms carries real relationship risk — reinforces the case for staying model-agnostic.
