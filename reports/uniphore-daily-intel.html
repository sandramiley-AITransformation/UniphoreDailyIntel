Today's Signal — five things worth knowing before your first meeting

  • Three days in, none of the four antitrust defendants has said a word. The Sept 18-19 class action against Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, and Google over alleged illegal "pacing" coordination still has no formal court response or spokesperson statement from any defendant as of this morning, even as coverage keeps expanding (Bloomberg Law, CNN, Fortune, CBS). Anthropic's own pre-IPO reporting now explicitly names the suit as a risk factor.

  • The CEO safety debate has turned personal — and the White House has picked a side. Jensen Huang said rival AI CEOs pushing "pace the frontier"-style warnings may have "ulterior, maybe political" motives and told reporters to "read between the lines"; CNBC reports Treasury Secretary Bessent says Trump is "completely aligned" with Huang, after Trump called AI-safety fears a "hoax" on stage with Huang at the All-In Summit. This is no longer a disagreement about tempo — it's an accusation of bad faith, backed by the presidency.

  • Zhipu's ZCode scandal just went from technical finding to commercial crisis. Enterprise customer Chengming Technology publicly demanded ZCode delete its uploaded project data over an alleged code leak, and reporting says multiple companies have halted use of the tool. Zhipu has committed to a third-party security audit and open-sourcing ZCode — the clearest sign yet that Chinese-model trust concerns are reaching real deployment decisions, not just benchmark debates.

  • Neocloud economics are visibly forking. CoreWeave upsized its convertible note offering to $3.7B (settling this Thursday) to keep funding buildout, while Nebius raised on-demand GPU prices as much as 21% and saw its stock jump 9-11% on the news — deepening scrutiny in one case, apparent pricing power in the other, from the same two companies last week's brief flagged for "circular financing" risk.

  • Model-routing infrastructure keeps attracting serious capital. Stripe's acquisition of OpenRouter is now a publicly confirmed, priced deal (~$7.5B, roughly 5.4x its Series B valuation three months earlier), and a16z separately led a $300M round into inference-routing startup Gimlet Labs at a $3B valuation — reinforcing that the "layer between customers and models" is being priced as strategic infrastructure, not commodity plumbing.
